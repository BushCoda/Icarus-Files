An independent community repository for researching, documenting, and supporting modding for the game Icarus.

This project brings together technical research, gameplay-system documentation, modding guides, extracted metadata, community projects, tools, and other useful resources for Icarus modders. The building-placement and grid systems are the initial areas of research, but this repository is intended to expand into additional game systems over time.

This is an unofficial community research project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by RocketWerkz or the creators of Icarus.

<h2 align="center" style="font-size: 1.6em;">Community Projects</h2>

This repository also serves as a directory for Icarus-related community work.

The [Community Project Showcase](COMMUNITY_SHOWCASE.md) features projects such as:

- Mods and gameplay modifications
- Building and construction projects
- Technical research
- Modding guides and tutorials
- Asset and data-extraction tools
- Utilities and development tools
- Experiments and proof-of-concept projects
- Other resources useful to Icarus players and modders

Featured projects remain in their original repositories and continue to be owned and maintained by their original authors. This repository provides a curated index and discovery point rather than copying or mirroring other people's work.

Want your project featured? See [How to Get Featured](COMMUNITY_SHOWCASE.md#how-to-get-featured).

<h2 align="center">Research Status</h2>

This repository is in its early stages. The building-placement and grid systems are the initial focus of research, but the long-term goal is to document as many useful Icarus systems as possible.

Future research areas may include:

- Building and construction systems
- Items, crafting, and recipes
- Characters, creatures, and AI
- Missions, prospects, and progression
- Inventory and storage systems
- Gameplay rules and world systems
- Networking and server/client behavior
- Blueprint and asset structures
- Data tables and configuration systems
- Modding tools and development workflows

Research findings should be read with their confidence and evidence in mind. Contributions should identify what was directly observed, what was inferred, and what still requires testing.
